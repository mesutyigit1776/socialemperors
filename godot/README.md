# Social Empires: Godot 4 port

A new client for Social Empires, written in GDScript for Godot 4.7. It does not contain Social Point files. You import the graphics and the game configuration from your own copy of the socialemperors server.

Status: the fourteenth milestone. The port shows one village map with its land, buildings, decorations and units, at the same positions as the original client. You can pan with a mouse drag and zoom with the mouse wheel.

You can buy buildings and decorations from the store (the village, defense, army, decoration, wonder and "New" categories) and place them on your own land. The store shows the items for the race of the map, as the original client does: it hides items until their store level, and it does not offer the items for "gold members", because the server starts the client without gold membership.

The "New" category is the store rotation of the server. The server offers 2 groups of items from `config/shop_rotation.json` for 48 hours, then 2 other groups, and the Halloween group on 31 October. The importer copies these groups and the shop settings from `server_config.json` to `shop.json`, and the port applies the same rules. It chooses the groups with its own random numbers, so in the same period it can offer other groups than the server. Only humans can buy the rotation items, as on the server. The port checks the map edges, the owned land, the other items (units also block a tile), the construction limits, the level and the resources. The construction limits are the same as in the original client: for example one market, one mine of each level, a number of houses that the town hall sets, and 10 towers for each land area. When the experience reaches the next level, the level goes up and the player gets the reward of that level, as in the original game. After each purchase it saves the village in the server format, with the previous version in `<save>.bak`. The server and the original client can load this save.

Click an item on the map to select it. You can move your own buildings and decorations, also the town hall, and sell them, except the town hall. You can turn them with the Turn button, and with the space bar while you place or move an item. A sale gives back a twentieth of the cost, in the resource of the cost. Items that cost cash give nothing back. The port does not move or sell units, trees and stone or gold deposits, as in the original game.

Units walk. Click a unit, then a free tile: the unit walks there around the other items. Click a mine, mill or farm to send a villager to work in it, or a tree, stone or gold deposit to harvest it. Select a building to take its units out. A harvest takes a few seconds and gives the resource; more villagers on the same resource give more. Gold deposits give more gold at higher levels. A harvested stone or gold deposit grows again after 3 hours. Buildings that train units, for example the town hall and the barracks, have a Train button. A training queue holds 5 units, and the units must fit in the population that your houses allow. Units of the other race on your map are not yours, as in the original client. Some units, for example the ghost, cost cash and food. The "Finish all" button ends the whole training queue at once for 1 cash for each started hour of training time that is left. The "Cancel" button takes the last unit out of the queue and gives back what you paid for it, as the original game does.

Units play the animations of the original graphics, for example the walk in each direction. A data folder from an import before this change has no animations, so its units show still images; import the files again to get them. Units walk at the speed of the original client: their velocity in pixels in each frame, with a short stop at each tile. Villagers walk faster when the town hall has a higher level, and faster again with a Fitness Centre or a Troll Coliseum. Units do not walk by themselves, as in the original client. An idle unit sometimes turns toward a neighbouring tile. When one of your units stands on a building other than a farm, or on another unit, it walks to a free tile near it. Saves from the server or the original client can have such units. The client moves wild animals in the same way but does not save the move, so the port keeps them in place.

Wild animals come to the village: sheep and cows for humans, also horses from level 11, and boars for trolls. At each start the port adds animals on the land that you do not own, until the map has 7 of each kind (6 horses), as the client does. The server counts the animals of the day in the save, and the client adds no more than 20 of a kind. When one of your units comes within 4 tiles of a wild animal, the animal becomes yours and walks into the nearest ranch of its kind; a horse walks into the Training Stables. When the ranch is full, the animal stops next to it; without a ranch, it walks to the town hall. Animals on your own land are yours when the village loads, as in the client. You can send your animals into buildings like other units.

Some buildings take units. A spearman, swordsman or archer that goes into the Training Stables with a horse in it takes the horse and becomes a knight or a mounted archer. The Nursery breeds a new animal every 9 hours from two animals of the same kind. The Sacrifice Altar takes any unit except siege units, and after one second the unit is gone and gives back a twentieth of its cost. Refuges take any unit, and the Fortress takes units that shoot; in the original game they protect these units in battles, which the port does not have yet. The Unit Warehouse keeps units outside the map and the population: select it to take a unit out or to buy one more place for 2 cash. When you sell a building, or a farm is removed, its units come out first, as in the client.

Mills, mines, farms, ranches and other production buildings produce a resource. A building with room for units works only with a unit in it: a villager in a mine, mill or farm, animals in a ranch. A ranch uses one animal for each collection. A mark shows the buildings that are ready, and a click on a ready building collects from it. The production times and amounts come from the game configuration. The mills and mines of humans work only after you choose a work time: 5 minutes, 1 hour, 4 hours or 8 hours, which give 0.5, 1, 2 or 3 times the amount. After a collection you choose again. Each unit in a building after the first one adds a fifth to the amount. A farm costs a third of its amount in wood at each collection. If you do not have the wood, the farm is removed, as in the original game. You can pay 2 cash to collect at once from a working mill, mine, restaurant, bakery, temple or wonder.

The Treasure Mine gives an item of a collection every 24 hours instead of a resource. A harvest of a tree, stone or gold also finds an item 4 times in 100. Only humans find items, as in the original game. The Collections button shows the items that you have. When you have each item of a collection, you can complete it and get its reward, for example a garden. You can also complete a collection for cash. The reward goes into the item store; the Item store button places it on your land.

Special buildings show their actions under the selection. The client gives every player a free Dragon Breeding Nest, and from level 8 a free Soul Mixer, when the map does not have one. The dragon nest and the Monsters Nest start for gold or cash. Then each care step makes the dragon or monster grow, and after it you wait 6 hours or pay a little cash. After 10 steps the unit comes out next to the nest, and the next one starts. The Rider Academy trains a dragon rider in 5 to 7 steps, with 15 hours or 15 cash after each step. A rider and a dragon of the rider's type go into Dragon Riding and become a tamed dragon. In the original game each training step and each taming needs a mini-game, which is a separate Flash file. The port does not have the mini-games and counts each one as won.

The Soul Mixer takes two units with a soul power. It chooses a new unit from their powers, at random, and you can pay cash for a stronger one. The mix takes the time of the new unit, which you can end with cash. Then the new unit and the two units come out. Units die only in battles, which the port does not have yet, but saves from the server can have dead units. The graveyard brings them back for potions, and sells 25 potions for 5 cash. The Heroes Grave brings heroes back for gold.

The Time Machine sells hourglasses that move the village 30 minutes to 24 hours forwards in time. A used hourglass moves all times of the save back, as the server does: production, training, quests, the nests, the Rider Academy, the temple, survival and the events. The shield, the help of neighbours and the daily bonus keep their times. The Weather Machine changes the style of the map: the land, the trees and the colour around the map. Grassy Meadows is free; the other five styles cost 20 cash each, once. After a use of either machine, the village loads again, as in the original game. The Supreme Dragon Temple gives the Supreme Bahamut Dragon after 12 steps, in any order: offerings of gold, stone, wood, food, mana and cash, the sacrifice of five dragons from the map (they do not go to the graveyard), and the Supreme Heart, the reward of a collection. After each step you wait 48 hours or pay 5 cash. The dragon then comes out next to the temple, and the temple starts again.

A new building that needs workers, for example the market or Mill II, gets only the player's own villager. The server also hires neighbours from its other saves, which the port cannot see. Select the building to hire each other worker for 2 cash, then open it; until then it does not work and takes no units, as in the client. Allies buildings need neighbours and wait for them.

The goals of the original game are in the port: the goal box at the top left shows the first two of the 205 goals that you did not finish, and a click opens the goal window with its picture, text, reward and progress. "DO IT" shows where to do the goal, as the client does: it opens the build menu, selects the building or unit, or opens the window that the goal needs. "Open Goal List" shows the 15 active goals. A goal counts what you do in the current session, also before it is active, and the building goals count the buildings on the map when the village loads. When an active goal is complete, you get its gold at once, and the save lists it as completed and rewarded, as the server does. The goals that need other players (attacks, neighbours, friends) stay open; the port does not have other players yet. The importer copies the pictures of the goals; import the files again to get them.

A new empire starts with the tutorial of the original game. A box at the top explains each step, and blue marks show where to click: select the town hall, train a villager, move it, cut a tree, build a house on the marked place, attack a troll with the knight, then the goal box leads through the first goals until three trees are cut. The client lets you do only what the step asks, and so does the port. At the end you get a dragon nest. The save keeps the last step, as the server does, and the tutorial does not start again.

The village has the other actions of the original game that the goals ask for. Click a land area next to your land to buy it for cash; the client sells land for gold only to players with neighbours. The Store button puts a building or decoration into the item store. The Rename button names your empire. The market trades 100, 200 or 300 wood, stone or food for gold, 20 times in 20 hours; the price goes up 2% for each purchase of a resource and down for each sale. The Unit packs button sells the packs of the configuration; each pack gives random units into the item store. Click a unit twice within one second to select your units of its kind near it, or press Select units and drag a box over your units; then click a place, a building or a resource for all of them. Fights in the village damage your buildings and units: they show a health bar until a villager repairs the building, 25 life each 2 seconds, or a healing spring heals the unit. The original game shows fire on damaged buildings; the port does not have its fire graphics yet.

The save changes are the same as from the server commands, with three differences. All follow what the client shows. When the first unit goes into an empty building (in the Nursery, the second), the port starts the production time then, as the client does on screen; the server does not change the time. When a unit comes out of a training queue, the port moves the start time of the queue to the next unit; the server keeps it, so after a reload all units of a queue come out at once. When you finish a training queue with cash, the port charges the price that the client shows and takes, which counts all units of the queue; the server charges only for the next unit. In one case the port follows the server, not the client: a market sale takes the gold price of the sale from the resource, as the server does, and not the amount that the client shows.

Battles have started. The port has the battle rules of the original client: units look for enemies, walk around buildings and attack, towers shoot, healers heal, and the damage, the timing and the walking speed are the same as in the client. You control your units as in the original game. Click a unit to select it, or click it twice within one second to select your units of the same kind near it, for example all archers. Drag from one of your units, or press the space bar and drag, to select the units in a box. Click the ground to send the selected units there in a formation, or an enemy to attack it. The selected items and the item under the mouse show a health bar.

The campaign quests are playable on all 28 islands of the island list: the troll and civilisation islands, the boss islands (Yeti, Golem, Rockdriller, two dragon islands), the Heroes island, the 7 hard islands and the 3 god islands. The Quests button opens the island window of the original game, with its pictures, buttons and fonts. The islands open one after the other, as in the original game; the window shows the stars that you won and a lock on the islands that you cannot start yet. Click an open island to sail there with all fighting units of your village (not the villagers and animals). Hard islands 6 and 7 and the god islands take a team of at most 20 units, which you choose in the team window; the god islands also need units of the group god or colossus. Choose 1 to 3 stars, at most one more than you won on the island; more stars give each enemy unit a copy. As in the original game, the battle already runs while the start window is open, but you can give orders only after the start; an enemy that dies before the start gives no gold. On a troll island, defeat all enemy units, then click the treasure to win. On a civilisation island, destroy the town hall. On a boss island, defeat the units of each part of the island and click its key; with all keys the boss comes. The other islands tell their goal in the start window. The bar at the top shows the gold and experience of the quest, and the Withdraw button ends the battle early. At the end, the result window shows the gold, the experience, the honour and the units that you lost: 60 percent of the units that died come back. The save then changes as the server command `end_quest` changes it, and the next island opens after a win. You can start 3 quests in a row, or 4 with the Odin statue; after 2 hours without a new quest you can start them again (the original game waits 6 hours; `quest/quest_book.gd` explains how to change this). The units use the special attacks of the original game. Select one of your units that has special attacks: its portrait buttons show at the bottom of the screen, with the time of the cast and of the cooldown, and the limit bar, which fills with the damage or the healing that the unit does. Click a button to use it; when the limit bar is full, the limit button starts the limit attack.

Spells work as in the original game. Human players get the spell book at the bottom left from level 15; troll players always have it. Click the book to see your spells, and the button under it to open the spell window, where you buy spells for gold or cash and see their levels. Click the mana to buy 5 mana for 20000 gold or 1 cash. Choose a spell, move the marker over the map and click to cast it; a right click, the Escape key or a click on the book cancels. Each cast costs mana and counts towards the next spell level (25 and 50 casts). In quests the 15 spells act as in the client, with two errors of the client that the port keeps or corrects; see `docs/GODOT_BATTLES.md`. In the village a cast costs its mana and shows the spell, but does not change your units, as summoned units are not saved there. When a player reaches level 15 in the village, the port gives a free Wizardry Academy, as the original game does; select it to open the spell window. The panel of the port is at the top right, so that it does not cover the book.

Enemies come to your village as in the original game. When the village loads, groups of enemy units stand on the land at the border of the map. From level 10, bandits come from the edge of the map and attack after 7 minutes; from level 15 and 20 more groups come, after 14 and 21 minutes. From level 25, in one of ten loads or new levels a siege comes, from level 30 a stampede of big mounts, and from level 35 an army; they attack after one minute. From level 25, the Evil Orc Dragon comes at each fifth level. When an enemy and your units or buildings are near enough to fight, or when an attack starts, the village shows as a battle: you control your units with the controls of the battles, towers shoot, and you can cast spells. The fight ends when it is calm. Each enemy unit that dies gives its experience and 5 gold; your units that die go to the graveyard. Destroyed buildings stay. Click an enemy in the village to start a fight, also with one of your units selected, which then attacks it. The original game shows the fights in the normal village, where you can build at the same time; the port shows the village as a battle until the fight ends.

Survival works as in the original game, from level 20: the Survival button opens the window of the survival maps. The first two maps can be played, as in the original game; the other three say "Coming soon". Each play takes one of three lives, which come back after 3 hours, and you can buy more lives with cash. The 30 units of your village with the strongest attack defend the relic in the middle of the map against waves of enemies from 8 points. After each four waves a boss comes, and while it lives, more enemies come from all points; at the end, kamikazes come without end. The play ends when the relic is destroyed. You keep all your units, the best time of the map goes to the save, and after 5 minutes or more you get a unit in the item store. The newest client hides the button of this mode; older versions showed it.

The weekly tournament of the original game is in the port without other players, from level 15: the Tournament button opens a window with the three bots of the tournament. Choose your team of up to 20 units and its formation in the team window of the original game; at most 10 dragons, gods and colossi, and some units cannot take part. Then start a match against a bot: both teams form, wait 10 seconds and fight, and you control your team. The match ends when one team has no units, or when you withdraw. The score is that of the original game: 50 for a win, and up to 25 for your units that are left and up to 25 for the bot's units that died. The original game ranked the players of the week on its server and gave prizes to the best ten; the port has no ranking and no prizes, and it does not keep the scores after you close the game. The configuration of this project has only the names and texts of the formations, not their layouts; the port places the units by these texts and the pictures of the formation buttons.

You can also try a battle on any quest map: start the port with `--battle <file>`, for example `--battle <socialemperors folder>/villages/quests/100000013.save.json`. All items of that map except the trees, rocks and other neutral items are enemies, and the units of your village start in the land area with the fewest items. Shots use the graphics of the original game and fly as in the client: arrows and rocks in a curve, rocks and cannonballs with smoke and a blast where they land, fireballs and other shots straight or in an arc. They start at the point of the graphic from which the original shoots, and aim at the height of the target, as the client does. A data folder from an import before this change has no graphics of shots, and its shots start and aim at the feet of the items; import the files again to get them. The health bars and the selection rings are simple shapes; their graphics are in the main file of the client, from which the importer takes only the windows of the quests. `docs/GODOT_BATTLES.md` has the plan for the next stages: spells, the other quests, special abilities and the single-player modes.

Not done yet: neighbours, other players and the collection islands. Help from neighbours, for example potions from friends and their help in the nests, is not done. The server has no command to buy more graveyard places, so the port does not offer it.

## Import the game files

You need Python 3 with the server requirements (`pip install -r requirements.txt` in the server folder). The import does not need Java or JPEXS.

```bash
python godot/importer/import_assets.py --source <socialemperors folder> --out <data folder>
```

- `--source` defaults to this repository.
- `--village <file>` selects a village save to import, for example `saves/<USERID>.save.json`. The default is `villages/initial.json`. You can give the option more than one time.
- By default the tool imports the land, the store buildings, all items of the store rotation, all units (bosses and abilities call units that are on no map), the Huge Egg of the second dragon island, the rewards that the special buildings and the collections give, and the items of the villages: the imported villages, the player saves in `saves/` and the quest maps in `villages/quests/` of the server folder. This includes the units in buildings, in the unit warehouse, in training queues, in the item store and in the graveyards. `--all` imports the graphics of all items. This takes a long time.
- The tool also imports the graphics of the shots and effects of battles from `assets/fx/`, of the spells from `assets/buildingsprites/magic_*.swf` and of the magic particles of the unit abilities from `assets/swf/20131010_magicParticles.swf`, copies the quest maps, and imports the windows of the quests (also the team window) and the spells from the client file `assets/flash/SocialEmpires1.4.07sec_fixed.swf` (`ui/windows.swf`, a copy without the parts that the port places itself), with their pictures, the banner of a cast from `magic_cast_marquee.swf`, and the alert of the spell book with its picture. An import before the alert shows the gift of level 15 only as a message. `spells.json` tells at which frame each spell acts. A data folder from an import before the spells has no spell book; the Quests button then asks you to import again.
- `--jobs <n>` sets how many graphics the tool imports at the same time (default 4).
- The tool does not draw the graphics. It copies the SWF file of each graphic to the data folder (`graphic.swf`), and the port draws the frames from it while it runs, see "Graphics" below. The import of the default set takes about 10 seconds; the data folder is about 830 MB. A data folder from an older import has PNG or WebP frames; the port then reports that the graphics are from an older import. Import the files again.
- The import can stop and continue. You can stop it with Ctrl+C, or it can stop for another reason, and continue it later: run the same command with the same `--out` folder. The tool skips each graphic and the windows that an earlier run finished, and imports the others. It imports a graphic again when its SWF file or the code of the tool changed, so after an update of the port the same command brings the data folder up to date. To start from the beginning, delete the data folder.

## Graphics

The port draws the art of the SWF files itself while it runs, with the extension `flash_art` (Rust, in `flash_art/`). It reads the shapes, the bitmaps, the nested sprites, the masks, the morph shapes, the color transforms, the filters (blur, glow, drop shadow, bevel, gradient glow, color matrix) and the blend modes of Flash. A frame is drawn the first time that the game shows it, at the zoom of the camera (scale 1, 1.5 or 2), and is then kept. When the zoom changes, the frames are drawn again at the new scale, so that the art stays sharp. `FrameCache` limits the memory of the frames to 512 MB; above it, it removes the frames used least recently that no node shows. The image of a frame covers only the part of the canvas that the frame draws on, so that the large units with big canvases need less memory. When a unit starts an animation, other threads draw its frames ahead. As in Flash, each nested animation plays on from the frame in which it was placed, also inside a sprite of one frame. The extension also reads the data of the frames that the game rules use: their bounds, the points where shots start, and the clips that units play.

Build the extension before you open the port. It needs Rust (https://rustup.rs):

```bash
cargo build --release --manifest-path godot/flash_art/Cargo.toml
```

The windows of the client come from the same extension: it draws their sprites, buttons and static texts, and it turns the fonts of their text fields into TrueType fonts for Godot.

`flash_art.gdextension` loads the library from `flash_art/target/release/`. The build is tested on Linux; for Windows, the cross build with `--target x86_64-pc-windows-gnu` compiles, but the port was not run on Windows or macOS. The examples of the crate help to compare the frames with other programs: `render` writes the frames of a symbol to PNG files, `dump` prints the display list of a frame, `layout` prints the data of the frames as JSON, `stage` writes a SWF file that shows one symbol, so that the Ruffle exporter draws the same frames, and `compare` compares the two sets of frames.

`docs/FLASH_ART.md` records the import failures, the differences from other renderers and the open items of the extension.

The frames are drawn at the size of Flash. The reference for the pictures is Ruffle (the version in `templates/js/ruffle/`): in a comparison of 428 graphics, 0.34 % of the pixels differ by more than 24 of 255. As in Ruffle, shapes are drawn with 4 samples in each pixel, and an object with filters is put on whole pixels. Blend modes such as overlay and multiply still mix only with the graphic itself, not with the map behind it.

## Run

Build the extension (see "Graphics"), then open the `godot` folder in Godot 4.7, or run:

```bash
godot --path godot -- --data <data folder>
```

Import the files again after an update of the port when it reports that a file in the data folder is missing; with the same `--out` folder, the tool skips the graphics that are still up to date. The port reads the data folder from `--data`, then from the `SE_DATA_DIR` environment variable, then from `user://imported`. Use `--village <file>` to open a different save, and `--screenshot <file.png>` to save an image and quit.

The port writes each change to the village file that it opened. To play a server save, give its path, for example `--village saves/<USERID>.save.json`. Do not run the server with the same save at the same time.

## Check the rules

`checks/village_check.gd` checks the placement rules, level-up, moving, selling, turning, collection, the special buildings, the goals, the tutorial and the save format without a window. It needs a data folder from an import of the unchanged socialemperors files, because the tiles and the expected values come from them. It writes its files to `user://checks/`.

```bash
godot --headless --path godot --script res://checks/village_check.gd -- --data <data folder>
```

`checks/battle_check.gd` checks the battle rules with fixed random seeds. It needs the same data folder for the stats of the units and buildings.

```bash
godot --headless --path godot --script res://checks/battle_check.gd -- --data <data folder>
```

`checks/quest_check.gd` checks the rules of the campaign quests: the island list, the quest limit, the save changes of a quest result, the sides, the stars, the goals, the units that come back, the keys and bosses, the dragon islands, the doors of the Heroes island, the team rules and the item rewards. It needs the same data folder, with the quest maps in `quests/`.

```bash
godot --headless --path godot --script res://checks/quest_check.gd -- --data <data folder>
```

`checks/spell_check.gd` checks the spell rules of the save (mana, buying spells and casts) and the 15 spells in battles, with fixed random seeds. It needs the same data folder, with `spells.json`.

```bash
godot --headless --path godot --script res://checks/spell_check.gd -- --data <data folder>
```

The exit code is the number of failed checks. Run the village check after you change the village rules or the save code, the battle check after you change the battle rules or the path search, the quest check after you change the quests, and the spell check after you change the spells.

`checks/quest_compare.gd` is not a check: it plays a quest with a plan of orders and prints the course of the battle, for the comparison with the original client, which `tools/ruffle_quest.js` plays in Ruffle. `docs/GODOT_BATTLES.md` ("Comparison with the original client") has the method.

## Layout

| Path | Contents |
|---|---|
| `importer/` | Import tool. `ui_import.py` imports the windows of the client; `swf_bounds.py` reads their bounds, text fields and placements, and the frame labels of the spells. |
| `flash_art/` | Rust extension that draws the frames of the SWF files and reads the data of their frames, see "Graphics" |
| `game_data/` | Game configuration (item and level definitions), and the construction limits and the collections that the client contains as fixed values |
| `village/` | Village save model and placement rules, map coordinates, path search, map view, camera and screen interface |
| `battle/` | Battles: the rules (the battle, its items, the unit AI, formations, shots, special abilities, and the spells in `battle/spells/`), `battle_view.gd`, which draws a battle, and `battle_screen.gd`, which runs it and turns the mouse into orders |
| `sprites/` | Loads the imported graphics of the items and of the shots and effects through `flash_art`. `clip_sprite.gd` plays the animation of a frame; `unit_sprite.gd` chooses the frame of a unit from its action and direction. |
| `village_attack/` | The enemies of the village: which come and where (`village_attacks.gd`), the fight in the village (`village_battle.gd`) and its screen, and the attacks between fights (`village_enemies.gd`). The group AI of the attacks is `battle/collective_ai.gd`. |
| `survival/` | Survival: the maps and their waves, the survival battle, and its windows. The AI of the enemies is `battle/ai/survival_ai.gd`. |
| `tournament/` | The weekly tournament: the match with its score, its screen and the window of the bots. The AI of the bot's team is `battle/tournament_ai.gd`. |
| `quest/` | Campaign quests: the islands, the island list rules, the quest battle with its goals and result, and the windows of the quests |
| `spell_book/` | The spell book, the spell window, the mana window, aiming and casting, and the graphics of the spells on the map |
| `ui/` | Shows the windows of the client that the importer imported (`ui_import.py`): their images, buttons, stars and text fields |
| `checks/` | Headless checks of the village rules, the save format, the battle rules, the quests and the spells |
